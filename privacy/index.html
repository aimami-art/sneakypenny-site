Privacy Policy

Last updated: 27 October 2026

This policy explains what information is handled when you play Sneaky Penny: 3D Puzzle (“the game”) on iOS or Android. The game is published by Finamap Yazılım ve Dan. Ltd. Şti. (“we”, “us”).

In short: the game needs no account. We do not ask for your name, email address, phone number, contacts, photos or location. Your progress is saved only on your device. The game shows ads through Google AdMob, some of them supplied by Unity Ads; they use your device’s advertising identifier, and where the law requires it, you are asked first, and you can say no. Unity, the technology the game is built with, receives the details of purchases and diagnostic reports, such as crash reports. If you allow notifications, the game can remind you of things in it and we can send you occasional announcements.

1. What is saved on your device

The game saves the following on your own device so that you can continue where you left off:

  • your progress (the levels you have reached);
  • your coins, hearts and boosters, and what you have bought in the game;
  • your settings, such as sound, music, vibration and language.

This information stays on your device. It is not sent to us and we cannot see it. It is removed when you delete the game or clear its data. It may be included in your device’s own backups (for example iCloud or Google backup), which are controlled by you and by Apple or Google.

2. What we do not collect

We ourselves do not collect, store or sell personal information about you through the game; the advertising described in section 4 is handled by Google and Unity. The game has no sign-up, no chat and no social features, and it does not read your contacts, photos, microphone, camera or location.

3. Purchases

Purchases made in the game are processed by the Apple App Store or Google Play, under their own terms and privacy policies. We never receive your card number or other payment details. The store tells the game that a purchase was completed, so that the game can deliver it.

The game’s purchase system, Unity In-App Purchasing, also sends Unity Technologies the details of each purchase and purchase attempt (the product, its price and currency, the transaction identifier and the store’s receipt), together with an identifier for the app on your device (on iOS, the identifier for vendor) and basic information about the device. Unity can also estimate the device’s approximate location from its IP address. Unity uses this as described in Unity’s Privacy Policy.

4. Advertising

The game shows ads through Google AdMob: a full-screen ad between levels from level 6 on, and, only when you choose to watch one, a rewarded ad that gives you a booster or a heart. Players who own Premium see no ads between levels.

To show and measure ads, Google may collect information from your device, such as its advertising identifier (the Advertising ID on Android, the IDFA on iOS), its IP address (from which an approximate location, such as your country or city, can be estimated), its model and settings, and how you interact with the ads. Google uses this as described in Google’s Privacy Policy and How Google uses information from sites or apps that use its services.

AdMob can also fill an ad slot with an ad from Unity Ads, an ad network of Unity Technologies, when Unity offers the best ad for it (this is called mediation). Unity Ads may then collect the same kind of information (your device’s advertising identifier, its IP address, its model and settings, and how you interact with the ad) to show and measure that ad, as described in Unity’s Game Player and App User Privacy Policy.

  • Your choice. In the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom, Switzerland and other places where the law requires it, the game asks for your consent before personalised ads are shown. This question is shown by Google’s User Messaging Platform, which uses your device’s IP address to tell which rules apply where you are and keeps your answer on your device. On iOS, the game asks for permission before your device’s advertising identifier is used for tracking. If you say no, you may still see ads, but they will not be personalised.
  • Changing your mind. Where the law requires it, the game’s Settings page has “Ad privacy settings”, where you can change your answer. You can also reset or limit your advertising identifier in your device settings (Android: Settings › Google › Ads; iOS: Settings › Privacy & Security › Tracking).
  • Reporting an ad. If you see an ad that seems inappropriate, tap “Report an ad” on the game’s Settings page or write to us, and tell us what the ad showed.

Apart from Google AdMob and Unity Ads, and the purchase details and diagnostic reports that Unity receives (sections 3 and 6), the game contains no other advertising or analytics tools.

5. Notifications

On its first launch the game asks whether it may send you notifications. If you allow them, the game can remind you when your hearts are full again and, if you own Premium, when your daily gift is ready. These reminders are set up on your device; nothing about them is sent to us.

We may also send occasional announcements, for example when new levels arrive. They are delivered by Firebase Cloud Messaging, a Google service. For this, once you accept the terms, Firebase gives your copy of the game a random identifier (a push token), and the game joins one list for all players and one for its language. The token does not tell us who you are. See Privacy and Security in Firebase.

You can turn notifications off at any time in your device settings.

6. Other companies involved

  • Apple and Google distribute the game. Depending on your device settings, they may share with us anonymous, combined statistics, such as the number of downloads and crash reports. These do not identify you.
  • Google provides the game’s ad system (Google AdMob), as described in section 4, and delivers our announcements (Firebase Cloud Messaging), as described in section 5.
  • Unity is the technology the game is built with. When the game runs, Unity may receive basic technical information about the device, such as its model and operating-system version. Unity also receives diagnostic reports, linked to an identifier for the game on your device: information about how the game runs and is used (such as how smoothly it runs and when it is started and closed) and, if the game crashes, a crash report with technical details about the error and the device. These reports help us find and fix problems. See Unity’s Privacy Policy. Unity handles the purchase details described in section 3 as well, and supplies some of the ads through Unity Ads, as described in section 4.

7. When you contact us

If you write to us, we receive your email address and whatever you include in your message. We use it only to answer you and to fix the problem you report. We do not share it with others, and we delete it when it is no longer needed or when you ask us to.

8. Children

The game is suitable for a general audience. We do not knowingly collect personal information from children. If you believe a child has sent us personal information, please write to us and we will delete it.

9. Your rights

Depending on where you live (for example under the GDPR in the European Union or the KVKK in Türkiye), you may have the right to ask what personal information we hold about you, to have it corrected or deleted, and to complain to your data protection authority. Since the game itself sends us nothing, this concerns only the messages you send us; for advertising, purchase and diagnostic data, see Google’s and Unity’s privacy policies linked in sections 3, 4 and 6. To use these rights, write to the address below.

10. Changes to this policy

If we change this policy, we will publish the new version on this page and change the date at the top.
